How the spillover numbers are built

  1. Universe. The 50 largest US-listed common stocks of US companies on NYSE/NASDAQ by TradingView market cap on the snapshot date. OTC foreign listings, preferred/depositary shares and second share classes (GOOG, BRK.A) are dropped.
  2. Reporters. Two groups: top-50 companies whose next earnings date falls in the 2-month window, and those whose latest results (EDGAR 8-K) came out in the 31 days before the snapshot. For a company that just reported, the "Latest result" card shows each peer's move on that result's reaction day.
  3. What the company reported. For each reporter, the latest earnings press release (8-K exhibit 99.1 on EDGAR) was read. Headline figures are copied from it, and the one-line summary and outlook are short paraphrases. Analyst consensus is not in the allowed sources, so the page does not call a result a beat or a miss unless the company itself says so.
  4. How to read the reaction. Rules applied to the measured moves: the own move vs SPY is compared with the stock's average report-day move over the 8 quarters: at least 1.5 times the average is "bigger than usual", 0.6 times or less is "smaller than usual". If at least 70% of same-sector peers moved the same way, the result is read as sector-wide news. At 30% or less it looks company-specific, possibly with money rotating into peers. Anything between is "mostly company-specific".
  5. Peers. The other top-50 names in the same TradingView sector. "Same industry" is flagged in the table as the closer business match.
  6. Past release times. The reporter's last 8 SEC EDGAR 8-K filings with Item 2.02 (Results of Operations). The filing's acceptance time tells whether results came before the open or after the close. When two such filings are less than 50 days apart, only the later one (the full results release) is kept, so pre-announcements and mid-quarter updates drop out. Banks file many 8-Ks, so their history is read from EDGAR's older filing pages too. ExxonMobil changed to a new holding-company CIK in 2026, so its older releases come from the previous CIK (34088). Berkshire publishes on Saturdays, so its reaction day is the following Monday.
  7. Reaction day. After the close (AMC) means the next trading day; before the open (BMO) or intraday means the same day.
  8. Excess move. Each stock's close-to-close return on the reaction day minus SPY's return that day, which strips out the general market move.
    excess = closed/closed-1 − 1 − (SPYd/SPYd-1 − 1)
  9. Pair statistics over the 8 quarters:
    • Same-direction rate: share of quarters where the peer's excess move had the same sign as the reporter's.
    • Correlation between reporter and peer excess moves.
    • Sensitivity: regression slope, i.e. peer move per 1% reporter move.

How to read the link labels

LabelRule (8 quarters)
Strong≥ 6 of 8 same direction, correlation ≥ 0.5 and sensitivity ≥ 0.3
Moderate≥ 6 of 8 same direction and correlation ≥ 0.4
InverseCorrelation ≤ −0.5: the peer tends to move the other way (often money rotating between groups)
WeakAnything else. 5 of 8 (62%) is close to a coin flip, so it does not count.

Limits

  • 8 quarters is a small sample. Labels are a starting point for questions, not a forecast or a recommendation.
  • A reaction day can also carry other news (macro data, the peer's own news). Nothing here separates those.
  • Sector is a broad filter. Real links run through customers and suppliers too (AMAT and LRCX sell equipment to chip makers in another sector), and those cross-sector links are not measured.
  • Sensitivity is measured on the reporter's stock reaction, not on its EPS surprise.
  • GEV daily closes are taken from the last hourly bar of each day (the daily series could not be saved in this run). In a 4-day check they differ from the official close by less than 0.1%.
  • AMD and AMGN reported after the close on 3 Nov; their reaction day (4 Nov) had not closed at the snapshot, so their latest result is not in the numbers yet.
  • TSLA, UNH and SPCX have no other top-50 company in their sector, so they have no peer spillover.
  • The "who reacts" notes on macro events are reasoning, not measured data.
